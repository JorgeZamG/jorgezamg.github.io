The Challenge

Customer service agents were spending significant time searching across disconnected sources — PDFs, internal wikis, SOPs, and live CRM data — to resolve customer issues. The process was slow, inconsistent, and highly dependent on individual experience. At the same time, the content team was bottlenecked by production volume. Writers spent most of their time drafting first versions, leaving limited space for strategy, iteration, and creative direction. In both cases, the core issue was not lack of knowledge — it was how that knowledge was accessed and used in day-to-day decisions..

My role spanned process design, prompt engineering, model evaluation, and user adoption — designing the layer between AI systems and the teams using them.

"The opportunity was not just automation — it was improving the speed and quality of decisions made by the people closest to the work."

Agents resolved customer issues without relying on supervisors for policy interpretation.

Teams reduced escalation dependency by enabling faster, more consistent judgment.

Copywriters shifted from drafting to directing — selecting and refining instead of starting from zero.

Content teams increased output without sacrificing quality or brand consistency.

Tool 01 — Customer Service Chatbot

How the Knowledge Chatbot Worked

The core problem was not intelligence — it was retrieval and context. Agents knew what they were looking for, but fragmented systems slowed them down. The goal was to enable agents to make correct decisions in real time, without switching between tools or relying on escalation. I worked with engineering to design a RAG-based system that unified all knowledge sources into a single conversational interface.

1

Knowledge Unification

Ingested PDFs, internal wiki pages, SOPs, and live CRM data into a vector store. Documents were chunked, embedded, and indexed — creating a single searchable knowledge layer across all sources for the first time.

2

Conversational Query Interface

Agents queried in natural language — "what's our return policy for damaged items over 90 days?" — instead of navigating separate systems. The chatbot retrieved relevant document chunks and synthesized a precise, sourced answer.

3

Live CRM Context

Integrated live customer data so agents could query order history, account status, and prior interactions alongside policy documents — giving full context in a single response rather than requiring tab-switching across systems.

4

Multi-Model Routing

Claude API handled complex synthesis and nuanced policy questions. GPT and Gemini were evaluated and selectively used for specific query types — optimizing for response quality and cost per interaction.

Tool 02 — AI Copilot for Content Operations

How the Copywriting Assistant Worked

The bottleneck in the content team was not ideation — it was throughput and drafting time. Writers were spending most of their effort producing first versions, limiting their ability to focus on strategy and refinement. The system was designed to shift writers from execution to supervision.

1

Brand Voice & Context Ingestion

I designed the prompting architecture — feeding the model brand guidelines, tone documentation, past high-performing posts, and campaign briefs. This gave the AI a working understanding of the brand's voice before generating any output.

2

Multi-Format Draft Generation

I built the prompt framework that structured how writers submitted briefs — the tool then generated multiple copy variants across formats (Instagram caption, tweet, long-form post) simultaneously. Multi-model approach allowed comparison of Claude, GPT, and Gemini outputs for tone and style.

3

Writer Supervision Layer

Copywriters reviewed, selected, and refined AI-generated drafts rather than starting from blank pages. Their role shifted upstream — focusing on brief quality, creative direction, and final judgment rather than volume production.

Faster
Content turnaround improved significantly — more posts shipped per week without increasing team size.
↑ Strategy
Copywriters redirected freed time toward campaign strategy and creative direction instead of drafting.

The Challenge

Customer service agents at a retail company were spending significant time searching across disconnected sources — PDFs, internal wikis, SOPs, and live CRM data — to resolve customer issues. The search was slow, inconsistent, and dependent on individual agent knowledge. Meanwhile the content team was bottlenecked producing social copy, with writers spending most of their time drafting first versions rather than thinking strategically.

My role spanned process design, prompt engineering, model evaluation, user adoption, and outcome tracking — the full non-engineering layer of both tools. The opportunity was clear: both teams had the domain knowledge to supervise AI outputs. Neither needed AI to replace their judgment — they needed AI to eliminate the low-value work upstream of it.

"My job was to design the layer between the AI and the people using it — making sure the tool fit the workflow, the prompts produced reliable output, and adoption actually happened."

Tool 01 — Customer Service Chatbot

How the Knowledge Chatbot Worked

The core problem was retrieval, not intelligence. Agents knew what they were looking for — they just couldn't find it fast enough across fragmented sources. I worked with the engineering team to design and deploy a RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation) pipeline that unified all knowledge sources into a single queryable layer, with Claude API handling synthesis and response generation.

1

Knowledge Unification

Ingested PDFs, internal wiki pages, SOPs, and live CRM data into a vector store. Documents were chunked, embedded, and indexed — creating a single searchable knowledge layer across all sources for the first time.

2

Conversational Query Interface

Agents queried in natural language — "what's our return policy for damaged items over 90 days?" — instead of navigating separate systems. The chatbot retrieved relevant document chunks and synthesized a precise, sourced answer.

3

Live CRM Context

Integrated live customer data so agents could query order history, account status, and prior interactions alongside policy documents — giving full context in a single response rather than requiring tab-switching across systems.

4

Multi-Model Routing

Claude API handled complex synthesis and nuanced policy questions. GPT and Gemini were evaluated and selectively used for specific query types — optimizing for response quality and cost per interaction.

Tool 02 — Copywriting AI Assistant

How the Copywriting Assistant Worked

The content team's bottleneck wasn't ideas — it was drafting volume. Writers were spending the majority of their time producing first-version social copy, leaving little bandwidth for strategy, creative direction, or quality refinement. The shift we aimed for: writers as editors and directors, not drafters.

1

Brand Voice & Context Ingestion

I designed the prompting architecture — feeding the model brand guidelines, tone documentation, past high-performing posts, and campaign briefs. This gave the AI a working understanding of the brand's voice before generating any output.

2

Multi-Format Draft Generation

I built the prompt framework that structured how writers submitted briefs — the tool then generated multiple copy variants across formats (Instagram caption, tweet, long-form post) simultaneously. Multi-model approach allowed comparison of Claude, GPT, and Gemini outputs for tone and style.

3

Writer Supervision Layer

Copywriters reviewed, selected, and refined AI-generated drafts rather than starting from blank pages. Their role shifted upstream — focusing on brief quality, creative direction, and final judgment rather than volume production.

Faster
Content turnaround improved significantly — more posts shipped per week with the same team headcount.
↑ Strategy
Copywriters redirected freed time toward campaign strategy and creative direction — higher-value work that AI couldn't do.

What Made Both Tools Work

🎯
Adoption depends on fitting the existing workflow. I mapped both teams' workflows before a single prompt was written — designing the tools to slot into how agents and writers already worked, not asking them to change. That's why both hit daily active usage.
🔀
Multi-model isn't just about cost — it's about fit. Claude, GPT, and Gemini each had different strengths. Routing by query type rather than defaulting to one model improved output quality meaningfully.
📐
The human role matters more when AI handles volume. Shifting agents and writers from execution to supervision only worked because they had genuine domain expertise. AI amplified their judgment — it didn't replace it.
🔁
Iteration with real users is the only way to tune. I ran structured feedback loops with both teams after launch — tracking usage patterns, surfacing friction points, and feeding changes back into prompt design and workflow adjustments. Final versions looked very different from first deploys.
